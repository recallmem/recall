Cost and speed

What Recall costs per user, per ticket or per player each month, how fast each call is, and how to spend less. Prices come from the price list and speeds from our own measurements, and every example shows its arithmetic, so you can redo it with your own numbers.

How pricing works

Recall runs on prepaid credit in US dollars, with no subscription; new accounts get $5. You pay for two things, learning from messages and answering questions. Everything else is included.

So a short message such as "Sounds good, book it" costs 50 × $1.75 / 1M = $0.0000875 to learn from, and a 400-token message 400 × $1.75 / 1M = $0.0007. A 30-token line from a bot you do not observe costs 30 × $1.75 / 1M = $0.0000525. Every stored message comes back with its tokenCount, so you can measure your own averages.

To keep a product fast: build prompts with turn, one request per turn; ask questions before the user needs the answer, when a conversation or scene starts, or stream the answer (chatStream) so the first words show sooner; and never wait for learning in the request path, because context already includes the newest messages. Bursts beyond your rate limit answer 429 with Retry-After; the SDKs wait and retry for you. Reasoning levels and models covers choosing a level.

Ways to spend less

Do not learn from your own agents. With observeMe: false on an assistant, bot or NPC, its lines count only what Recall reads, with no 50-token minimum and at most 200 tokens as context, and memory fills with facts about your users rather than about your prompt. Short lines and long replies save the most.

Turn reasoning off where you only need context. A chat with a visitor who is not signed in, or a pasted log or document: set reasoning: { enabled: false } in the configuration of the session or of that one message.

Read context instead of asking. If your model writes the reply anyway, context gives it what Recall knows, faster and at no charge.

Ask at the lowest level that answers well. minimal costs about a sixth of low; max costs 50 times low.

Keep answers instead of asking again. Recall caches answers until the memory behind them changes, but a cached answer is still billed. Keep the answer in your app for the conversation, and refresh it when a queue.empty webhook says learning for that session has finished.

Batch messages. Store a turn's messages in one call (turn, or addMessages with several), and store a streamed reply once it is complete, not chunk by chunk. If your users send bursts of short lines ("hi", "quick question", "about my order"), consider joining them into one message: the 50-token minimum applies to each message Recall learns from.

Set a monthly cap. Under Billing, set a monthly cap so a bug or a leaked key cannot spend more than you planned, and auto top-up so learning does not pause when credit runs low. The dashboard's Usage page shows charges per day.

Pitfalls

  • Asking on every turn. A question at low per reply adds $0.005 and seconds to every turn. Read context instead and ask only when your code needs an answer.
  • Learning from your own bot. Leave observeMe on for an assistant and Recall profiles your prompt, and every "Got it" counts as 50 tokens.
  • Storing streamed chunks as messages. Each chunk becomes a message. Store the reply once it is complete.
  • Waiting for learning in the request path. waitForIdle is for tests and scripts. In production read context, which includes the newest messages at once.
  • max by default. It costs 50 times low and is slower. Keep it for the questions that need it.
  • No cap on a public app. A retry loop or a leaked key can spend all your credit. Set a monthly cap before launch.
